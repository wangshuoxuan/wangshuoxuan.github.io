body_zh: '我花了大约一年不断尝试，才找到一套比较适合自己的生活习惯。以前，我对健康的管理远没有现在这么稳定。如今，我感觉更舒服，工作状态也更稳定，每天用来为前一晚的行为补救的时间，也少了许多。


  适合你的生活习惯，很可能与我的不同。睡眠、饮食、运动和工作环境，都值得你亲自尝试、调整。你可能因此完成更多事情，也可能过得更开心，更有自由决定如何度过一天。仅凭这一点，这些尝试就值得。


  我粗略估计，坚持这些习惯时，我的效率比不做时高出约1到1.5倍。我没有实际测量过，也说不清每项习惯分别贡献了多少。但在平常的一天里，我能明显感到差别。


  我也希望自己能长久地保持健康。我还有很多想学、想做的事，也希望能亲眼见到未来世界的许多变化。


  ## 睡眠


  ### 保持规律作息


  我尽量一周七天都保持相同的作息。对我来说，睡七到七个半小时通常比较舒服。我一般在晚上十点半到十一点左右入睡，早上六点到六点半起床，周末与工作日没有太大区别。


  对我更有用的一条规则，是晚上十点前回到房间。我需要时间准备睡觉，让自己慢慢安静下来。有一次，我和朋友玩桌游到快十一点，回去时又晚了一些，以为自己还可以像平常一样入睡。结果，我一直很兴奋，过了午夜才睡着。晚归对我的影响，远不止在外面多待的那段时间。


  如果某天睡晚了，我会尽快恢复平常的作息。一次破例，很容易变成新的就寝时间。跨时区旅行时，我也会提前安排睡眠，而不是等到了当地，再想该怎么办。


  我用Sleep Cycle记录睡眠，但通常不设早晨的闹钟。我醒来后几乎不赖床。对我而言，自然醒比被闹钟从睡梦中叫醒舒服得多，尤其是在需要临时早起的时候。如果身体需要多睡一点，我更愿意为它留出余地。


  我也试过日出唤醒灯。它会在设定的时间开始亮起，用大约三十分钟逐渐变亮，像在床边模拟一次日出。但我后来发现，它对我的用处不大。我睡得离窗户很近，醒来直接拉开窗帘就好。现在，无论是声音闹钟还是灯光闹钟，我都更愿意用自然醒来代替。


  我租的房间朝南。起床后的第一件事，就是拉开窗帘。晨光会让整个房间舒服很多；如果天还没亮，我就开灯。出门吃早餐，再走路或骑车去图书馆，让我在开始学习前有一段待在户外的时间。我不喜欢整个上午都留在自己睡觉的房间里。


  ### 睡眠环境与睡前过渡


  我在安静、黑暗、偏凉的房间里睡得最好。我晚上戴耳塞，但不戴眼罩。遮光窗帘对我更有用，我也会遮住不必要的指示灯。我不喜欢房间太热，也不喜欢被子过厚、盖着太暖。


  我买了一个小白噪音播放器，通常放在枕边，用低音量播放雨声。我觉得这个声音很舒服。它给了我的注意力一个简单的落点，让我不必跟着脑海里每一个冒出来的念头走。


  我在睡前停止工作这件事上，做得没有那么好。我还没能稳定做到睡前一小时放下手机、不工作、不看社交媒体。最近，我在尝试用听书代替看屏幕，最好是一些平静、有点难，或者略显枯燥的内容。


  如果我已经困了，脑子却还在转，继续想下去通常没什么帮助。我会提醒自己，明天还可以接着处理，再把注意力放回呼吸或雨声。有时，做一点冥想也有用。我希望自己能在二十分钟左右入睡，但如果一直盯着时间，就又多了一件让我担心的事。


  大约半年来，我也会在睡前半小时到一小时服用褪黑素。刚开始时，困意更明显，现在没有那么突出。我没有察觉到明显的副作用，但这只是我的个人体验，不能证明长期使用安全，也不能解释为什么效果发生了变化。


  ### 一个自己的住处


  我搬出了宿舍，每天花大约120元住在校外。最初主要是为了睡眠，现在我白天的大部分时间也仍然在别处。但这个房间的用处，远不止睡觉。


  整个空间都由我自己使用。我可以把东西放在自己想放的位置，洗衣服、晾衣服，自主安排房间和时间，不必每一个细节都和室友协调。这让我感觉像有了一个自己的小家。


  住在合用的房间里，别人的晚睡、灯光和噪声，都会成为你作息的一部分。我觉得反复协商很累。在这里，我可以决定一天什么时候结束，房间保持什么样子。我愿意付这笔钱，很大一部分也是为了这份自由。


  如果你在宿舍睡得舒服，和室友也相处融洽，你可能完全没有必要做同样的取舍。但对我来说，能自主安排自己的空间，值得这笔开销。


  ### 午睡


  我通常会给自己留出二十分钟午睡。如果下午较早有课，我就把午睡提前。否则，我一般一点多吃完午饭，一点二十左右回到图书馆，再走二十分钟左右，在一点四十五到两点左右开始休息。


  我会带上耳塞、眼罩和一个小靠垫。我不喜欢趴在桌上睡：脖子弯着，头也发沉，醒来后有时仍然昏昏沉沉。我更喜欢有靠背和宽扶手的沙发，最好靠着墙。我把靠垫放在内侧，让自己稍微半躺，头颈都有支撑。这比直接靠在硬椅背上，更接近我平常熟悉的睡姿。


  回宿舍午睡，来回都需要时间，到了也未必安静。别人的动作或一点声音，就可能把我吵醒；躺上床，也不一定更容易快速入睡。只是短暂休息，在图书馆通常更省事。如果找不到合适的位置，我就闭眼休息，或者做一小段非睡眠深度休息（NSDR）。即使没睡着，休息本身仍然有帮助。


  我把手机设成二十分钟后振动。这样不用响亮的闹铃就能叫醒我，也不会打扰周围的人。我不靠猜测判断自己睡了多久。睡三四十分钟，我往往不如短睡后清醒；睡一个小时，有时感觉更差。如果确实需要长一点的休息，我会留出约一个半小时，另找一张床躺下。我不认为九十分钟就一定对应一个完整的睡眠周期。


  醒来后，我会先洗把脸、走一走，再开始学习。我也尽量不在午睡前紧接着喝茶。上午犯困时，我通常先试试散步、拉伸，或换一项轻松的任务，而不是再加一次小睡。我一般一天只午睡一次，之后尽量让自己在整个下午和晚上保持清醒。


  ## 食物与饮品


  ### 一份可以长期重复的早餐


  我在早上七点到八点吃早餐，中午十二点半到一点左右吃午餐，晚上七点左右吃晚餐。我尽量在七点半前吃完晚饭。周末也基本如此；确实有其他安排时，我才调整，而不是每天重新决定吃饭时间。


  早餐通常是燕麦，再搭配鸡蛋、牛奶、无糖豆浆、坚果、莓干、奇亚籽或即食鸡胸肉中的几样。巴旦木是我常吃的一种坚果。我希望蛋白质和脂肪足够，吃得饱，上午又不会发沉、犯困。


  我很少吃食堂里的蛋糕、甜点、甜饼干、馒头、包子和类似的精制面食。作为学生，我能自己处理食物的条件有限，所以方便很重要。有几个月，我一直把燕麦麸皮放进杯子，加温热水拌匀后直接吃。我现在正在核对具体产品的说明，寻找明确适合这种吃法的产品。我后来才发现，燕麦麸皮和钢切燕麦并不是同一种东西。


  ### 午晚餐，以及少做一些决定


  午晚餐时，我会多要蔬菜、少要白米饭。我会更换蔬菜的种类和颜色，肉类优先选择鸡肉和鱼肉，少吃红肉。有米线或糙米时，我也经常选它们。如果有清淡的汤和小份水果，我也很愿意加一份。我一般先吃蔬菜，再吃肉，最后吃主食。


  我尽量避开很辣、很油或很甜的食物，尤其是晚餐。食堂有不少菜不适合我，所以我会在几个熟悉、喜欢的窗口之间换着吃。这比每顿重新做决定，再要求自己克制，要省力得多。


  食堂的分量通常让我吃得刚刚好。我晚餐吃得少一些，之后一般不再进食。我通常会婉拒夜宵，让晚饭和睡觉之间留出三个多小时。这样的安排，让我的肠胃更舒服。


  我每周和朋友约饭一到两次，其中大约一次在校外，也会提前说明自己不吃辣。吃饭的节奏很重要。在中餐厅，几道菜可能一起上齐，我很容易因为菜一直摆在眼前，就不停地吃。如果请服务员一道道上，像分道供应的一餐，中间就自然有了停顿。我能吃得更平缓，也不容易在察觉自己已经饱了之前，就吃下太多。


  吃得太快，会让我肠胃不舒服，也会影响后面的食欲。坐在桌边一个半小时，断断续续地吃，同样好不到哪里去。我更喜欢一小时左右吃完，再提议一起散步。如果肠胃已经不舒服，但我们还想继续聊，我就站起来，而不是一直坐着。我们可以继续聊天，不必继续吃。


  ### 咖啡、茶与加餐


  早餐后八点左右，我会用约四克隅田川冻干咖啡粉，加三百毫升水，冲一杯黑咖啡。我不加糖。有时，我会加牛奶，尤其是凉牛奶；比起纯黑咖啡，我更喜欢拿铁的味道。有几次，喝完甜咖啡，我恰好在准备开始学习时犯困。


  我买原味茶叶或茶包。我通常在九点、十点左右喝绿茶或红茶，有时喝乌龙茶，控制在两杯以内，并在中午前结束所有含咖啡因的饮品。我不希望上午的咖啡因一直影响到晚上。


  午睡后，我可能会泡大麦茶、姜枣茶或路易波士茶。在北京的秋冬，我尤其喜欢这些茶。它们让我感到舒服，也让午睡后重新进入下午的状态更轻松。晚上，我有时会喝洋甘菊茶或茯苓茶，通常在九点左右，不喝太多。


  其他时候，我主要喝矿泉水。无论学习还是旅行，我都尽量不买甜饮料。聚会时，我会偶尔破例；享受当下的气氛也很重要。但我仍然尽量不在晚餐喝酒，因为我不喜欢酒精对睡眠和第二天早晨状态的影响。


  上午十点左右，我可能会吃一点黑巧克力，通常选可可含量约80%的。含量低很多，我觉得太甜；高很多，又太苦。有时，我会换成坚果或香蕉。下午三四点，我会吃小份水果或坚果，偶尔吃一块饼干、一片巧克力，或一些即食鸡胸肉。如果确实要吃甜食，我更愿意放在午餐前后，而不是上午。


  ## 运动


  ### 我实际做些什么


  运动不是这套习惯里我做得最好的一部分。我没有在某个特定项目上投入很多，也很少参加球队或校内比赛。我更喜欢有时间就能独自完成的活动。和朋友定期训练当然很好，我只是不喜欢每次想动一动，都必须约定固定的地点和一群人。


  散步是我坚持得最稳定的活动。我早餐后会走一会儿，午晚餐后各走二十分钟左右，有时慢走，有时快走。我也骑车在校园里通勤。其他时候，我可能慢跑、划船，或出于好玩去体验一项不熟悉的活动，不追求每项都练得很好。


  没有体育课的学期，我通常每周去三到四次健身房，每次三十到四十分钟。我一般安排在傍晚六点到七点，尤其是六点半以后、晚餐之前。我会结合有氧和力量训练：划船机、快走、骑行、举重、一些拉伸，偶尔短距离冲刺。清晨也是我会考虑的时间，但傍晚更容易坚持。晚饭后，我就以散步为主。


  每周进行一百五十分钟中等强度有氧活动的建议，对我来说是一个有用的参考。但我去了几次健身房，并不自动等于达到了这个标准；还要看持续时间和强度，举重和拉伸也属于不同的活动。散步和骑行，同样是我保持活动的一部分。


  ### 让运动容易坚持


  如果身体疲惫或生病，我会减少活动，或者休息。但脑子累，不一定意味着身体也累。经过一天高强度的学习，活动身体有时更像休息，而不是又多了一项负担。


  我坐得开始发僵、酸痛或发麻时，也会站起来活动。小幅度的动作和短暂的散步很容易被忽略，但它们不需要太多准备，就能融入一天的生活，所以更容易坚持。刚开始健身可能不太舒服，但散步通常从一开始就让人感觉不错。


  我没有遇到过度训练的问题。如果说有什么倾向，我反而对强对抗和受伤比较谨慎。一次骨折，就可能打断很多我在意的事情。相比主要为了保持健康而参加的运动，我更愿意在智力工作上承担风险。


  ## 我学习工作的地方


  ### 留住状态最好的时间


  上午是我状态最好的时间，其次是下午两三点到六点。我尽量不往这两个完整时段里插入其他安排。会议和其他事情，能放在午餐前后或六点以后，我就尽量放过去。每天能有几个不被打断的小时，我一整天都会从容很多。


  我会在可以自主安排的时候，让日程保持相对宽松。对于那些自学或看回放效果更好的课程，我更愿意在图书馆完成。我不想仅仅因为某件事可以被排进日程，就把一个状态很好的上午用在它上面。


  ### 光线与熟悉的环境


  我通常会坐在图书馆同一个安静的角落，最好在南侧、靠近自然光。我喜欢清楚东西放在哪里，也能方便地把自己的物品放在身边。学习工作本身已经有足够多的新问题，我不需要每天早晨再适应一个不同的环境。


  我喜欢明亮的房间，窗帘拉开，但没有刺眼的阳光或屏幕反光。博雅塔附近有些较暗的位置，晚上会让我犯困，所以我会避开。自然光让我感觉更清醒，心情也更好。出去待一小会儿，同样有帮助。


  我试过在桌边养绿植，但总记不住照顾它们。相比之下，看窗外的绿意更可靠。有时，最好的调节就是走出去，看看校园，感受一下当天的天气。


  ### 让自己舒服一些


  图书馆的椅子比较硬，所以我买了坐垫和一个软靠背。按我遇到的管理规定，我不能带自己的工学椅，但可以改善现有的椅子。如果有自己的工位，我会有更多选择。


  我会在电脑支架能让姿势更舒服时使用它，不舒服时就把电脑拿下来。处理困难的技术问题时，我通常坐得比较端正；写作或思考时，我往往更放松。我不觉得一整天维持某种所谓完美的姿势，对自己会有什么帮助。


  大约每过一小时，或当我发现注意力开始飘走时，我就起身走一走。有时，我在桌前停住的思路，会随着身体的走动重新动起来。


  ### 声音与自己的空间


  我在桌前放一块纯白挡板，挡住来回走动的人，减少视野中的干扰。角落的位置和挡板，围出一个让我觉得熟悉、私密的小空间。不觉得自己正被别人看着时，我工作起来更舒服。


  突然的声音，比持续的背景声更让我难受。椅子刮地、物品撞到桌面，都可能打断我的思路；空调安静运转的声音，反而能盖住一些零碎声音，帮助我安静下来。我通常戴泡棉耳塞，必要时再加一副头戴式隔音耳罩。对我来说，泡棉耳塞最舒服。


  我也更喜欢独自工作。和朋友一起学习，有时能帮助我完成任务，但过一段时间，我就开始觉得自己应该一直显得很忙。如果我想暂停一下、换个姿势，或者短暂做点无关的事，就不如独处时自在，仿佛需要解释自己在干什么。


  如果你参加过人人都开着电脑的组会，你可能就知道这种感觉。周围都是敲键盘的声音，我也会不自觉地找些简单的小任务，让自己一直有事做。我可能完成了一大堆小事，却完全没碰那个真正需要认真思考的难题。缓慢的思考和开放式的写作，从旁边看未必显得有效率。当我不需要每隔几分钟就展示一点进展时，我反而工作得更好。


  这需要一定的自律。和同伴在一起效率更高的人，可能并不需要我这样强的独立空间。我喜欢能够换个姿势、停一停，或者短暂做一点无关的事，不必把它变成需要大家一起决定的事情。


  ## 补充剂


  ### 我目前在用什么


  我目前服用十几种补充剂，不包括咖啡和茶。这是在记录我一直在做什么，并不是一份我认为所有人都应该照着吃的清单。这也是整套习惯里，我最没有把握的一部分：比起大多数补充剂，我更清楚睡眠、食物和咖啡因对自己的影响。


  早上喝完咖啡，我会吃一粒茶氨酸，希望自己的状态平静一些。在高强度的学习日，有时也在考试前，我会用Alpha-GPC，大约每周四五天，而不是每天都吃。任务轻时，我就不用。它和咖啡因一起使用，有时会让我感觉刺激过强，包括心跳加快。这已经超过了我想要的兴奋程度。


  我也用过L-酪氨酸，通常是在睡得不好、早起、熬夜或倒时差让我很疲惫，却仍然要应付高强度一天的时候。我希望靠它度过一段短期压力，但无法单独判断它到底帮了多少。对上午的状态，咖啡因仍然是我最容易察觉到作用的那一种。


  午餐后，我会补充维生素D3和K2、B族维生素，以及Omega-3鱼油。如果当天吃的水果很少，我可能也会补充维生素C。傍晚运动、吃完晚饭后，我会把三到五克肌酸一水合物粉末兑水喝下。我也在晚饭后服用假马齿苋（Bacopa monnieri，也叫婆罗米），希望长期对记忆有所帮助，但我不能说它已经明确改善了我的工作记忆。


  睡前几小时，我会补镁，临近就寝时再服用前面提到的褪黑素。有些肠胃不舒服的时期，有时是吃得油腻之后，我还加过益生菌，偶尔额外补充维生素。我仍然需要更清楚地弄明白，其中哪些有用，为什么用。


  ### 我还不清楚的部分


  我总体感觉身体不错，也很少生病，所以很容易想把功劳归给补充剂。但我同时改变了好几件事。感觉好，不能说明是哪一种补充剂起了作用；没有察觉问题，也不能证明这个组合安全。我已经感受到的过度刺激，是让我更谨慎看待叠加使用的理由，不能证明这是一套适合别人的习惯。


  我更看重Omega-3和咖啡因，而不是收集一长串产品。其他许多补充剂的边际收益，我还不清楚。我更愿意弄明白自己实际需要什么，而不是只因为某个解释听起来合理，就不断往清单上加东西。


  ## 压力与人际关系


  ### 把事情从脑子里拿出来


  我的很多焦虑，都来自一件重要、却还没有开始做的事。第一步是认清它。如果能开始，哪怕只做一点，我通常也会好受一些。如果暂时做不了，把它写进清单也有帮助。一旦写下来，我就不必一直提醒自己还有这件事。


  我把清单当作一个外脑。它帮我记住任务和需要留意的事情，我就不必同时把一切装在脑子里。疲惫时，我可能会换一本轻松的书、找人聊聊，或做点自己感兴趣的事，而不是一直逼着自己推进同一项任务。


  我一天通常能做四五个小时真正高强度的工作，其余时间可以轻一些。我不指望把所有清醒的时间都放在图书馆或实验室，不运动、不见朋友、没有自己的兴趣，长期下来还能把工作做得更好。


  ### 独处，但不与外界隔绝


  我喜欢独处，也认为在随时可以聊天、刷信息的时代，这是一项值得学习的能力。散步和阅读，是我最喜欢的独处方式。活动身体让我感觉更有活力，也常常更容易思考。我喜欢根据自己的状态，换一条路、换一个环境，暂时没有人需要我处理什么事。


  阅读历史和传记，是我暂时离开眼前烦恼最好的方式之一。我不断在那些过着截然不同生活的人身上，找到熟悉的感受。有些人很有名，很多人并不是。读他们的经历，有时就像跨越时间，认识了一个能理解我部分处境的人。


  我看电子书、听书和播客，也关注一些网上社群与新闻，喜欢科技深度报道、长文、长视频和课程。我几乎不看短视频。它们当下可能很有趣，但看完后，我往往觉得自己花了时间，却没留下多少东西。长一点的内容，更能让我感到充实。


  ### 给关系留出时间


  我尽量每天联系一下朋友，但不一定见面。可能是聊几句，发条消息问问近况，或者帮一点小忙。我不要求这些交流产生什么结果。帮到别人、收到他们的回应，本身就让人开心。


  我在大学里关系较近的朋友，大多来自信科和物理学院，尤其是信科。我们很容易理解彼此，但这个圈子也会渐渐变得可预测。我希望认识其他院系、其他学校的人，接触不同的经历和思考方式。


  社团对我来说有适合的地方，也有不适合的地方，因为它们固定的活动时间，我不一定能长期配合。约咖啡、下午茶，或从微信上的聊天慢慢转到线下，都更灵活。但共同做事仍然很重要，社团也能创造这样的机会，我不想否定它的价值。


  我会参加有意思的活动，有时也自己组织小范围的聚会：吃饭、旅行、桌游、散步，或者在公园度过一个下午。有时大家原本就认识，有时我会把不同院系的朋友约到一起。我希望接触更多不同的人，而不只是让通讯录里多几个名字。


  ### 我还没有解决的部分


  在大学维持友谊，比我预想的更需要花心思。大家都忙，理工科高强度的学习科研尤其如此。如果没人主动，很容易就一直见不到。有固定见面机会的关系，比如课上经常遇到的同学，就轻松得多。下课聊一会儿、一起吃午饭，比每次重新约时间省力。


  我也喜欢朋友带上他们的朋友。这能让圈子自然地向外延伸，偶尔也会让我遇到一个真正合得来的人。


  我还在摸索，怎样让关系更稳定，也更相互。我喜欢关心别人，也希望有人关心我。一条消息、一次邀请，或者有人来找我帮忙，对我都可能很有意义。总是由自己发起，确实会消耗精力。我希望有更多关系，不必一直刻意安排，关心也能自然而然地双向流动。

  ### 轻断食

It took me about a year of trying things to find a routine that works reasonably well for me. I used to be much less consistent about my health. I now feel better, work more steadily, and spend less of the day trying to recover from something I did the night before.

Your routine will probably look different from mine. Sleep, food, exercise, and the place you work are worth experimenting with. You may get more done, but you may also feel happier and have more freedom to choose how you spend the day. That alone makes the effort worthwhile.

My rough guess is that I'm 1–1.5× more productive when I keep up these habits than when I don't. I haven't measured that, and I couldn't tell you how much each habit contributes. But the difference in an ordinary day feels large.

I also want to stay healthy for a long time. There is a lot I want to learn and do, and a lot about the future I would like to be around to see.

## Sleep

### Keeping regular hours

I try to keep the same sleep schedule throughout the week. Seven to seven and a half hours usually feels right for me. I generally fall asleep around 10:30 or 11 and wake between 6:00 and 6:30. Weekends are not very different from weekdays.

The more useful rule is to be back in my room by 10. I need time to get ready for bed and settle down. Once I played board games until almost 11, got back a little later, and assumed I could still fall asleep more or less as usual. Instead, I stayed excited and didn't sleep until after midnight. Getting home late costs me more than the time spent out.

If I have a late night, I try to return to my usual schedule soon afterward. It is easy for one exception to become the new bedtime. I also plan sleep when traveling across time zones, rather than treating it as something to deal with after arrival.

I use Sleep Cycle to keep track of sleep, but usually don't set a morning alarm. I almost never stay in bed after waking. Waking naturally feels much easier to me than being pulled out of sleep by an alarm, especially for an unusually early start. If I need a little more sleep, I prefer to leave room for it.

I also tried a sunrise alarm lamp. At a set time it would gradually brighten over about 30 minutes, a little artificial sunrise beside the bed. I didn't end up finding it very useful. I sleep close to a window and can simply open the curtains when I wake. These days I prefer waking naturally to being woken at a set time, whether by sound or by light.

My rented room faces south. The first thing I do after getting up is open the curtains. The morning light makes the room a much nicer place to be. If it is still dark, I turn on a light. Breakfast and the walk or bike ride to the library give me some time outside before I start working; I don't like spending the whole morning in the room where I slept.

### The room and the wind-down

I sleep best in a quiet, dark, fairly cool room. I wear earplugs at night, but not an eye mask. Blackout curtains work better for me, and I cover unnecessary indicator lights. I don't like a hot room or an overly warm duvet.

I bought a small white-noise player and usually leave it on a quiet rain setting near my pillow. I find the sound comfortable. It gives my attention somewhere simple to go instead of following every thought that turns up.

I am less good at stopping work before bed. I haven't consistently managed the advice about putting away my phone and avoiding work or social media for the last hour. Lately I have been trying audiobooks instead of looking at a screen, preferably something quiet, fairly difficult, or a little dull.

If I am sleepy but still thinking, more thinking rarely helps. I remind myself that I can return to the problem tomorrow and bring my attention back to breathing or the rain. Sometimes a little meditation helps. I would like to fall asleep within about 20 minutes, but watching the clock would give me one more thing to worry about.

For roughly six months I have also used melatonin about half an hour to an hour before bed. The sleepiness felt stronger at first and is less obvious now. I haven't noticed clear side effects, but that is only my experience; it doesn't establish long-term safety or tell me why the effect has changed.

### A place of my own

I moved out of the dorm and spend about RMB 120 a day on a place off campus. Sleep was the main reason, and I still spend most of the day elsewhere. But the room is useful for much more than sleeping.

The whole space is mine to use. I can keep my things where I want, do laundry, hang clothes to dry, and arrange the room and my time without having to coordinate each detail with a roommate. It feels like having a small home of my own.

In a shared room, other people's late nights, lights, and noise become part of your schedule. I found the repeated negotiations tiring. Here I can decide when the day ends and what the room is like. The freedom is a large part of what I am paying for.

If you sleep comfortably in a dorm and get along well with your roommates, you may have no reason to make the same trade. For me, having control over the space has been worth it.

### Naps

I usually make time for a 20-minute nap. If I have an early afternoon class, I move it earlier. Otherwise I finish lunch after 1, return to the library around 1:20, walk for another 20 minutes or so, and rest around 1:45 or 2.

I carry earplugs, an eye mask, and a small cushion. I don't like sleeping with my head on the desk: my neck is bent, my head feels heavy, and I can come up feeling groggy. I prefer a sofa with a back and a wide armrest, ideally beside a wall. I put the cushion on the inner side so I can recline a little with my head and neck supported. It feels more like my usual sleeping position than resting against a hard chair back.

Going back to the dorm adds a trip in each direction, and the room may not be quiet when I get there. Someone else's movements or a sound can wake me, and being in bed doesn't necessarily make it easier to fall asleep quickly. For a short nap, the library is usually less trouble. If there isn't a suitable seat, I close my eyes or do a short non-sleep deep rest session. Resting still helps when I don't fall asleep.

I set my phone to vibrate after 20 minutes. It wakes me without a loud alarm and doesn't disturb people nearby. I don't rely on guessing how long I have slept. Thirty or forty minutes often leaves me less clear-headed than a short nap; an hour can feel worse. If I really need a longer rest, I allow roughly an hour and a half and find a bed instead. I don't assume that 90 minutes is a guaranteed sleep cycle.

Afterward I wash my face and walk a little before trying to work. I also avoid having tea immediately before the nap. On a sleepy morning I usually try a walk, a stretch, or an easier task first, rather than adding another nap. I normally nap once, then stay awake for the rest of the afternoon and evening.

## Food and Drinks

### A breakfast I can repeat

I eat breakfast between 7 and 8, lunch around 12:30 to 1, and dinner around 7. I try to finish dinner by 7:30. Weekends are much the same; I change the times when there is a real reason to, rather than starting over every day.

Breakfast is usually oats with some combination of eggs, milk, unsweetened soy milk, nuts, dried berries, chia seeds, or ready-to-eat chicken breast. Almonds are a regular choice. I want enough protein and fat, something filling, and a morning without a heavy, sleepy feeling.

I eat few of the cakes, pastries, sweet biscuits, steamed buns, and similar refined-flour foods at the canteen. There isn't much I can prepare as a student, so convenience matters. For a few months I mixed oat bran with warm water in a cup and ate it that way. I am now checking the instructions on the actual product and looking for one explicitly suited to this preparation. Oat bran and steel-cut oats turned out not to be the same thing.

### Lunch, dinner, and fewer decisions

At lunch and dinner I ask for more vegetables and less white rice. I vary the kinds and colors of vegetables, prefer chicken and fish, and eat red meat less often. I often choose rice noodles or brown rice when available. A mild soup and a small serving of fruit are welcome additions. I generally eat vegetables first, then meat, then the starch.

I avoid very spicy, oily, or sugary food, especially at dinner. Many canteen dishes don't suit me, so I rotate between a few stalls I know and like. This saves more effort than trying to make a fresh decision, then exercise restraint, at every meal.

Canteen portions usually leave me comfortably full. Dinner is smaller, and I don't normally eat afterward. I generally decline a late-night meal so there are more than three hours between dinner and bed. My stomach is happier with that arrangement.

I eat with friends once or twice a week, roughly once off campus, and mention in advance that I don't eat spicy food. The pace matters. At a Chinese restaurant, several dishes may arrive together, and I can keep eating simply because everything is there. Asking for dishes to come one at a time, more like a meal served in courses, gives me a pause between them. I eat more steadily and am less likely to get through too much food before noticing that I am full.

Eating too quickly can make my stomach uncomfortable and put me off the rest of the meal. Staying at the table and eating on and off for an hour and a half isn't much better. I prefer to finish in about an hour and then suggest a walk. If my stomach feels uncomfortable and we still want to talk, I stand up rather than keep sitting. We can carry on the conversation without carrying on the meal.

### Coffee, tea, and snacks

After breakfast, around 8, I make black coffee with about 4 grams of Sumidagawa freeze-dried coffee and 300 milliliters of water. I don't add sugar. Sometimes I add milk, especially cold milk; I like the taste of a latte more than plain black coffee. Sweet coffee has sometimes been followed by a sleepy patch at exactly the point I wanted to start working.

I buy plain tea leaves or tea bags. I usually have green or black tea around 9 or 10, sometimes oolong, no more than two cups, and finish all caffeinated drinks before noon. I don't want the morning's caffeine following me into the night.

After my nap I might make barley tea, ginger and jujube tea, or rooibos. I particularly like these in Beijing's autumn and winter. They are comforting and make the transition back into the afternoon nicer. In the evening I sometimes have chamomile or poria tea, usually around 9, and not too much.

Otherwise I mostly drink mineral water. I try not to buy sweet drinks, whether I am studying or traveling. I make exceptions at gatherings; enjoying the occasion matters too. I still avoid alcohol with dinner because I don't like what it does to my sleep and the next morning.

Around 10 I may have a little dark chocolate, usually about 80% cocoa. Much less tastes too sweet to me; much more is too bitter. Sometimes I have nuts or a banana instead. Around 3 or 4 I have a small serving of fruit or nuts, occasionally a biscuit, a piece of chocolate, or some ready-to-eat chicken. If I do have something sweet, I prefer it around lunch rather than in the morning.

## Exercise

### What I actually do

Exercise is not the part of this routine I am best at. I haven't put much into a particular sport and rarely join teams or campus competitions. I prefer things I can do on my own, when I have time. Regular training with friends can be great; I just don't like committing to a fixed place and group every time I want to move.

Walking is the most consistent thing. I walk a little after breakfast and about 20 minutes after lunch and dinner, sometimes slowly and sometimes briskly. I also cycle to get around campus. At other times I might jog, go rowing, or try an unfamiliar activity for fun, without trying to become good at all of them.

In semesters without a PE class, I usually go to the gym three or four times a week for 30 to 40 minutes. The usual slot is between 6 and 7 in the evening, especially after 6:30, before dinner. I mix cardio and weights: the rowing machine, brisk walking, cycling, lifting, some stretching, and occasionally a short sprint. Early morning is another time I would consider, but evenings are easier to keep. After dinner I stick to walking.

The guideline of 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week has been a useful reference. My gym visits don't automatically add up to that; duration and intensity matter, and lifting and stretching are different activities. Walking and cycling are also part of how I stay active.

### Keeping it easy to continue

If I am physically tired or ill, I do less or rest. But a tired mind is not always a tired body. After a demanding day of studying, moving can feel like a break rather than another demand.

I also get up when sitting starts to feel stiff, sore, or numb. Small movements and a short walk are easy to overlook, but they fit into the day without much preparation. That is why they last. Going to the gym can feel unpleasant at first; a walk usually feels good from the beginning.

I haven't had an overtraining problem. If anything, I am cautious about hard contact and injuries. A fracture could disrupt a lot of things I care about. I am more willing to take risks in intellectual work than in a sport I am doing mainly to stay well.

## The Place I Work

### Protecting the good hours

Mornings are my best time, followed by the period from about 2 or 3 to 6 in the afternoon. I try to leave both blocks alone. Meetings and other arrangements go around lunch or after 6 when possible. Having a few uninterrupted hours makes the whole day feel less hurried.

I keep the schedule fairly open when I can. For courses where independent study or a recording works better, I prefer doing that in the library. I don't want to use a good morning on something merely because it can be put on a calendar.

### Light and familiarity

I tend to use the same quiet corner of the library, preferably on the south side near daylight. I like knowing where everything goes and being able to put my things around me without a fuss. There are enough new problems in the work itself; I don't need a different setting every morning.

I like a bright room, with the curtains open but without harsh sun or reflections on the screen. Some of the dimmer seats near Boya Tower make me sleepy in the evening, so I avoid them. Daylight makes me feel more awake and puts me in a better mood. Going outside for a little while helps too.

I tried keeping plants beside my desk, but wasn't good at remembering to care for them. Looking at greenery outside the window is more reliable. Sometimes the best adjustment is just to step outside and notice the campus and the weather.

### Being comfortable

The library chairs are hard, so I bought a seat cushion and a soft back support. I couldn't bring my own ergonomic chair under the rules I encountered, but could improve the chair that was there. At a desk of my own, I would have more options.

I use a laptop stand when it makes the position more comfortable, and take the laptop off it when it doesn't. For a hard technical task I tend to sit upright; for writing or thinking I am often more relaxed. I don't think holding one supposedly perfect pose all day would help me.

About once an hour, or whenever my attention starts drifting, I get up and walk. Sometimes the thought that wasn't moving at the desk starts moving with me.

### Noise and privacy

I put a plain white divider in front of the desk to keep passing people out of my field of view. The corner seat and the divider make a small space that feels familiar and private. I work more comfortably when I don't feel watched.

Unexpected sounds bother me much more than a steady background hum. A chair scraping or something striking the table can break a train of thought; an air conditioner running quietly can cover other noises and help me settle in. I usually wear foam earplugs and add over-ear hearing protection if needed. The foam plugs are the most comfortable for me.

I also prefer working alone. Studying with friends can help me finish tasks, but after a while I start feeling that I should always look busy. I have less room to pause, change position, or do something unrelated for a moment without explaining myself.

If you've sat through a research group meeting with everyone on their laptops, you may know the feeling. There is typing all around you, and I find myself reaching for small, easy tasks that let me keep moving too. I can get a lot of those done without touching the difficult thing I actually need to think about. Slow thinking and open-ended writing don't always look productive from the next chair. I work better when I don't feel the need to demonstrate progress every few minutes.

This takes some self-discipline. Someone who does better with company may want much less separation than I do. I like being able to change position, pause, or do something unrelated for a moment without making it a group decision.

## Supplements

### What I have been using

I currently take around a dozen supplements, not counting coffee or tea. That is a description of what I have been doing, not a list I think everyone should copy. This is also the least settled part of the routine: I have a much clearer sense of what sleep, food, and caffeine do for me than of what most of the pills do.

After my morning coffee I take one L-theanine capsule, hoping for a calmer feeling. On demanding study days, and sometimes before an exam, I also use Alpha-GPC, roughly four or five days a week rather than every day. On easier days I leave it out. With caffeine, the combination has sometimes felt too stimulating, including a faster heartbeat. That is more arousal than I want.

I have also used L-tyrosine on occasions when poor sleep, an early start, a late night, or jet lag left me tired but I still had a demanding day ahead. The intention was to get through a short period of pressure; I can't isolate how much it helped. Caffeine is the one whose effect on my morning is easiest to notice.

After lunch I take vitamin D3 with K2, B vitamins, and omega-3 fish oil. If I have eaten little fruit that day, I may also take vitamin C. After my evening workout and dinner, I mix 3 to 5 grams of creatine monohydrate powder into water. I also take Bacopa monnieri after dinner, with the hope of improving memory over time, although I can't claim a clear improvement in working memory from it.

A few hours before bed I take magnesium, and melatonin closer to bedtime as described above. During periods of stomach discomfort, sometimes after oily meals, I have added probiotics and occasionally extra vitamins. I still need a better account of which of these are useful and why.

### What I don't know yet

I have generally felt healthy and haven't been ill much, which made it tempting to credit the supplements. But I changed several things at once. Feeling well doesn't show which supplement helped, and not noticing a problem doesn't prove that a combination is safe. The stimulation I have felt is a reason to be more cautious about the stack, not evidence that it is a good routine for someone else.

I care more about omega-3 and caffeine than about collecting a long list of products. For many of the rest, the marginal benefit is unclear to me. I would rather find out what I actually need than keep adding things because the explanation sounds plausible.

## Stress and Other People

### Getting things out of my head

A lot of my anxiety is an important task I haven't started. The first step is to identify it. If I can begin, even doing a small part usually makes me feel better. If I can't, putting it on a list helps. Once it is written down, I don't have to keep reminding myself that it exists.

I think of the list as an external memory. It holds tasks and things to watch so I don't have to hold them all at once. When I am worn out, I may switch to an easier book, a conversation, or something I am interested in rather than keep forcing the same task.

I can usually do about four or five hours of genuinely demanding work in a day. The rest can be less intense. I don't expect spending every waking hour in a library or lab, with no exercise, friends, or interests, to give me better work over the long run.

### Being alone without feeling cut off

I like being alone, and think it is a skill worth learning when a conversation or a feed is always available. Walking and reading are my favorite ways to spend that time. Moving makes me feel more alive and often makes thinking easier. I like choosing a different route or setting according to how I feel, with nobody needing me for a while.

Reading history and biography is one of the best ways I know to step outside the worries of the day. I keep finding familiar feelings in people who lived very different lives. Some are famous; many aren't. Reading them can feel like meeting someone across time who understands a part of what I am going through.

I read ebooks, listen to books and podcasts, follow some online communities and news, and enjoy long technology reporting, essays, videos, and courses. I almost never watch short videos. They can be enjoyable in the moment, but I tend to come away feeling that I spent time without keeping much. Longer material is more satisfying to me.

### Making room for people

I try to contact friends every day, though not necessarily in person. It might be a short chat, a message to see how someone is doing, or a small piece of help. I don't need those conversations to produce anything. Helping someone and hearing from them feels good in itself.

Most of my close university contacts are in information science and physics, especially information science. We understand each other easily, but the circle can become predictable. I want to meet people from other departments and universities, with different experiences and ways of thinking.

Clubs are a mixed fit for me because their regular meeting times can be hard to keep. Coffee, afternoon tea, or a conversation that begins on WeChat and moves offline are more flexible. Still, doing things together matters, and clubs create occasions for that which I wouldn't want to dismiss.

I join interesting events and sometimes organize small groups myself: a meal, a trip, a board game, a walk, or an afternoon in a park. Sometimes everyone already knows each other; sometimes I bring together friends from different departments. I want a wider mix of people, not just more names in my contacts.

### The part I haven't solved

Friendship at university takes more effort than I expected. People are busy, especially with demanding science and engineering work. If nobody initiates, it is easy not to meet. The friendships with a built-in occasion—someone I see in class, for example—are much easier. Talking after class and getting lunch together costs less effort than arranging every meeting from scratch.

I also like it when friends bring their friends along. It lets the circle grow naturally, and occasionally introduces someone I really connect with.

What I am still working out is how to make relationships more steady and mutual. I like checking in on people, and I like being checked on. A message, an invitation, or someone asking me for help can mean a lot. Always being the person who starts things takes energy. I would like more relationships where care travels both ways without so much arranging.

## Other Things I've Tried

### Intermittent fasting

I tried a 16:8 eating schedule for two or three weeks. The first food was around 11, then lunch around 1 and dinner around 7. It did not work well for me.

Some mornings I felt unusually clear around 8 or 9, but later became foggy, sleepy, and physically drained, sometimes achy. By 11 I could feel quite bad. I described it at the time as low blood sugar, though I didn't measure it. Eating did not fix the feeling immediately and sometimes left my stomach uncomfortable too. I was usually back to normal after the afternoon nap, and evenings were fine. Losing the best part of the morning was a poor trade, so I stopped.

### Making the easy choice easier

I am not vegetarian. Meat is something I want in my diet. I try to limit sweets, but I like them and don't expect to win an argument with myself every time one is within reach.

Keeping snacks beside the desk makes it easy to eat them without noticing. The simplest solution has been not buying them, not leaving them in the place I work, and not ordering dishes I already know I would rather avoid. Occasional exceptions with friends are easier to live with than a desk that asks for restraint all day.
